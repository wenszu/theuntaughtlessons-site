Your recording script

Put this where you can read it while recording. Aim for calm authority with short pauses, a steady pace and no rush through the turns.

The Olympics is one of the few remaining promises the world still recognizes. Excellence can be earned, witnessed and remembered by anyone, from anywhere.

That is why this matters.

And that is why we should be honest.

The problem is not awareness or popularity. The problem is that the Games are losing their hold on people.

The world used to gather around the Games because there was no substitute. Now people can choose from more stages, feeds and events than they can follow. Shared moments are harder to create. Stories are compressed into clips, and commercial messages can drown out the human stakes.

The Olympics does not need more noise. It needs to give people a reason to care and come back.

We believe that happens through three moves.

First: Rebuild the ritual. Design shared moments the world can actually enter together, not “catch up on.”

Second: Make the meaning clear. Use athlete-led story arcs that restore stakes, context, and consequence over time.

Third: Create one consistent direction. Give casual viewers a clear reason to understand what matters, why it matters, and who to care about without losing prestige.

We will respect cultural limits and remain neutral across markets. We will also account for sponsors and broadcast rights. The plan must be precise enough to work within all four constraints.

In the next sixty to ninety days, we will propose one pilot that proves the Games can win people back through live return, story retention, and trust.

The Olympics should once again feel like an open stage where human limits become history. Thank you, everyone, and let the Games begin.

Record and upload

  1. Set up your phone: Open your camera in portrait video mode. Keep the phone vertical, prop it at eye level, and make sure your face and shoulders are visible.
  2. Put the script near the lens: Keep the script close enough to read without looking far away from the camera.
  3. Record one complete take: Use the timer if helpful. Pause between sections and keep the final line clean.
  4. Check sound and framing: Play the first few seconds. If the audio is muddy or the framing distracts, rerecord once.
  5. Prepare your prompt, then open Gemini: Use "Prepare AI feedback prompt" below, copy it, and paste it into a new chat in your own Gemini, before you upload anything.
  6. Upload your recording: In that same Gemini chat, attach the video or audio file. If needed, move it from phone to laptop with AirDrop or Google Drive. Gemini accepts video up to 2GB and up to 5 minutes total, so this short recording fits with room to spare.
  7. Use the feedback for one better take: Read what Gemini sends back, pick the one thing to fix first, then rerecord with that one change in mind.
