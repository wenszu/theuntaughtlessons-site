Step 1 of 3 · Understand the approach

Use AI to hear several useful perspectives before you decide.

A virtual advisory board is a set of AI-generated advisors, each assigned a specific role. For example, one advisor can think like a customer while another can test whether the idea will work. You can choose other roles based on the question you need to answer. A board chair compares their advice and recommends what to do next. You make the final decision.

A simple example

Where should a school hold its end-of-year celebration?

The answer changes when the question is examined through distinct perspectives.

Without a board

Choose the park because it is fun and inexpensive.

This may be reasonable, but it does not show which needs or risks were considered.

With a virtual advisory board
Student representativeWill students enjoy it and want to attend?
Parent representativeIs it safe and affordable for families?
Event organizerIs there enough capacity and a weather backup?
Board chairRecommend the park only if a nearby indoor backup is reserved.

How does a virtual advisory board work?

What is a virtual advisory board?

A set of AI-generated advisors, each assigned a specific role. One might think like a CFO. Another might think like a customer. Another might think like someone responsible for making the project work. AI is useful here because you can tell it which role to take and what that role should examine. You can also ask AI to recommend a suitable mix of advisors that would bring different perspectives and useful insights to your question, then review or change its suggestions. You receive several distinct reactions to the same decision instead of one general answer.

Why do multiple roles matter?

Most people ask AI one broad question and receive one broad answer. Giving AI a role changes what comes back. A CFO notices things a customer may miss. A customer notices things an operator may miss. Clear roles make the feedback specific enough to compare, challenge and use. You can weigh each response, push back on it and decide what applies.

Who is the orchestrator?

The orchestrator is the board chair, not another advisor. The chair does not add another perspective to the list. The chair listens to the advisors, compares what they said and decides what matters most. This turns several opinions into one recommended direction.

Why does someone need to make the final call?

More advice can create more confusion. The advisors widen the picture. The board chair orders the advice and recommends a decision. Without someone responsible for that step, you can end up with a long list of things to fix and no clear priority. You remain responsible for the final call.

How do I choose my orchestrator?

You can choose someone who thinks like you when the decision is yours to own. You can also choose the person best suited to the problem, even if that person thinks differently from you. Ask whether you need a chair who represents your judgment or one whose experience best matches this specific problem. Those are not always the same choice.

How do I get the most out of this?

Give the board something real to react to. In this exercise, you will provide a clear question, the context the board needs and your starting view. Each advisor responds from an assigned role. Review the comments rather than accepting all of them. Decide which feedback improves the decision and which feedback you can set aside.

Can I include a well-known person?

Yes, but AI cannot reproduce what a real person would say. A well-known name is shorthand for a broadly associated style, such as Steve Jobs on simplicity. Use an established professional role when you need technical or factual judgment.

What should I do with the board's advice?

The board helps you see angles you may have missed. You still own the decision. Check any important claims, especially when money, health, law or safety is involved.

Step 2 of 3 · Build the board

Choose who should examine the problem.

Start with the question and context. Then choose three or four board members whose perspectives will help you think through the solution. One member will serve as the board chair.

Choose useful perspectives

The three recommended members cover simplicity, the audience, and delivery. Select any card to add or remove it. Keep the board focused with no more than four members.

Well-known names work best when you name the perspective you want. Role-based experts work well when the decision needs specific technical judgment.

2c

Choose the board chair

The chair compares the different views and recommends one direction. You will review it and make the final decision.

AI can help assemble the board

You do not need to know every board member before you begin. Give AI your question and ask which perspectives would help you examine it from different angles.

Prompt to try: “Which three perspectives would help me examine this decision? Explain what each person should focus on, what assumptions they should challenge and why that perspective belongs on the board.”

You can use professional roles, such as a customer researcher, financial advisor or operations leader. You can also use well-known people as shorthand for a recognizable style of thinking. Review the suggestions and replace any advisor who would repeat a perspective already represented.
