# Brand Guidelines – The Untaught Lessons

## Core identity
- Clean, minimal, and thoughtful
- Feels intelligent but simple
- Focus on clarity over cleverness
- Every element must help understanding or action
- No decorative elements

## Visual tone
- Calm, structured, and practical
- Feels like an executive whiteboard, not a marketing site
- Prioritize function over aesthetics

---

## Colors

### Primary colors
- Primary Navy: #003366 (main text, headers)
- Primary Gold: #EEA320 (actions, highlights only)
- Brand Cream: #F3EDE2 (main background)

### Supporting colors (limited use)
- Charcoal: #4A4A4A (secondary text)
- Muted Steel: #4D7094 (subheadings, subtle accents)

### Rules
- Follow 60-30-10:
  - 60% cream background
  - 30% navy text
  - 10% gold accents
- Gold is ONLY for emphasis or action
- No bright or additional colors

---

## Typography

### Titles
- Font: Playfair Display
- Weight: Bold or Semi-bold
- Color: Primary Navy

### Body
- Font: Lato
- Weight: Light or Regular
- Color: Charcoal

### Technical / AI elements
- Font: Roboto Mono
- Color: Gold or Charcoal

### Rules
- Large, readable headers
- Strong hierarchy
- Avoid dense text blocks

---

## Layout principles

- Strong whitespace (do not fill space unnecessarily)
- Left-aligned text
- Clear section separation
- Simple vertical flow (no complex grids)

### Structure pattern (default page)
1. Header (title + short description)
2. Primary content (tools or ideas)
3. Supporting sections
4. Minimal footer

---

## UI rules (IMPORTANT for Claude)

- Buttons:
  - Background: Primary Gold
  - Text: Navy or white
  - No gradients or effects

- Sections:
  - Use spacing instead of lines
  - Use cream background always

- Containers:
  - Use subtle contrast (champagne/parchment if needed)
  - Avoid borders unless necessary

- Navigation:
  - Simple and minimal
  - No complex menus

---

## Visual style (critical)

- Use hand-drawn, sketch-like visuals when possible
- Lines should feel slightly imperfect (not geometric-perfect)
- Use charcoal (#4A4A4A) for drawings
- Use gold ONLY to highlight key insight

### Do not:
- Use stock illustrations
- Use polished corporate graphics
- Use 3D or gradients

---

## Content tone

- Clear, calm, practical
- Written to help thinking, not impress
- Short, structured, and direct
- No corporate jargon

---

## Do

- Keep pages simple
- Make tools easy to use immediately
- Prioritize readability and clarity
- Remove anything unnecessary

---

## Do not

- No flashy animations
- No bright colors
- No cluttered layouts
- No decorative elements without function
- No marketing-style exaggeration

---

## Default homepage expectation

- Clean title and subtitle
- Clear list of tools
- Minimal text
- Immediate usability
- No visual noise

---

## Product family (multiple products, one system)

UTL is not one product. Each tier signals its weight through restraint, not new color.

- **Flagship (TSA today, one at a time):** the only tier allowed the navy gradient, glow and centered spotlight treatment. This exception exists to make the flagship program read as the single biggest commitment on the page. No other product gets this treatment, ever.
- **Discover (the readiness assessment, and future free tools):** the plain brand system exactly as written above. Cream card, navy headline, gold action, left-aligned, its own small hand-drawn mark (`assets/mark-readiness.svg` is the pattern to copy). Smaller and quieter than the flagship by design, not by accident. The assessment's mark has five points because the assessment measures five areas, hand-drawn rather than geometric because the result is personal, not a fixed category, and on the results page the same five points redraw themselves to the person's own scores, so it doubles as a one-glance summary of their shape, with the gold point marking their strongest area.
- **Add-on (one product's own upsell, like the assessment's facet report):** stays gold-fill like any primary action, because inside its own flow it is the primary action. What changes is a level down: a flagship cross-link surfaced inside another product's result (the facet report closing with "join the TSA waitlist") stays gold-outline or plain link, quiet by comparison, so the product the visitor is actually using still reads as the payoff, not a detour.

Every product gets its own mark (never a stock icon), reuses navy/gold/cream/charcoal/steel exactly as defined above, and never invents a new palette. If a fourth tier is ever needed, extend this list before inventing a new visual language.
